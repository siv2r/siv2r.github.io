Transforms move mass, not points

A transform of a random variable moves probability mass, and the only honest way to follow it is to track a small segment, never a point. This is the dxdxdx view from pdf-and-cdf-basics doing real work.

The warm-up was W=−ZW = -ZW=−Z. My flipping argument: negation literally maps z=−kz = -kz=−k to +k+k+k, so the PDF flips over 0, and φ\varphiφ is even, so W∼N(0,1)W \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)W∼N(0,1) too. The crypto anchor: Gaussians carry lattice crypto. Falcon samples discrete Gaussians, LWE hides secrets under Gaussian noise, and the security arguments are tail bounds, the 68-95-99.7 rule taken to cryptographic extremes.

The Exponential from its survival function

The Exponential Expo(λ)\text{Expo}(\lambda)Expo(λ) models the waiting time until the first arrival when arrivals hit at rate λ\lambdaλ. My recall worry ("doesn't e−λxe^{-\lambda x}e−λx blow up on the left?") is answered by the story: waits are never negative, the support is (0,∞)(0, \infty)(0,∞), and the blow-up region is simply not in it. The kernel e−λxe^{-\lambda x}e−λx needs a constant, and unlike the Normal's 2π\sqrt{2\pi}2π​ this one is a freshman integral: c=λc = \lambdac=λ, so

f(x)=λe−λx,x>0.f(x) = \lambda e^{-\lambda x}, \quad x > 0.f(x)=λe−λx,x>0.

Units sanity check: λ\lambdaλ carries per-time units, exactly the density units a PDF over time must have, and λx\lambda xλx in the exponent is dimensionless.

The CDF is F(x)=1−e−λxF(x) = 1 - e^{-\lambda x}F(x)=1−e−λx, so the survival function is P(X>x)=e−λxP(X > x) = e^{-\lambda x}P(X>x)=e−λx. That survival function is the real primitive: exponential-survival-first rebuilds it from nothing but a constant rate, and everything below runs through it.

The mean and variance come from the chapter's signature move, compute for the simplest family member and transform out. One integration by parts gives E(X)=1E(X) = 1E(X)=1 for Expo(1)\text{Expo}(1)Expo(1), and taking E(X2)=2E(X^2) = 2E(X2)=2 on faith gives Var(X)=1\text{Var}(X) = 1Var(X)=1. Memorylessness

Memoryless property A continuous distribution on (0,∞)(0,\infty)(0,∞) is memoryless if for all s,t≥0s, t \ge 0s,t≥0: P(X≥s+t∣X≥s)=P(X≥t)P(X \ge s + t \mid X \ge s) = P(X \ge t)P(X≥s+t∣X≥s)=P(X≥t) Given you have already waited sss without success, the chance of surviving another ttt is a fresh start. The past sss is erased.

The proof is two lines once you see the collapse: the event X>s+tX > s+tX>s+t implies X>sX > sX>s, so their intersection is just the smaller event, and the conditional becomes a ratio of survivals:

P(X>s+t∣X>s)=e−λ(s+t)e−λs=e−λt.P(X > s+t \mid X > s) = \frac{e^{-\lambda(s+t)}}{e^{-\lambda s}} = e^{-\lambda t}.P(X>s+t∣X>s)=e−λse−λ(s+t)​=e−λt.

What the whole proof rests on, in one sentence: exponential functions have the property f(x+y)=f(x)⋅f(y)f(x+y) = f(x) \cdot f(y)f(x+y)=f(x)⋅f(y). The survival function factorizes, so conditioning divides the past out exactly. No polynomial or Gaussian tail survives that ratio unchanged. And the converse holds: any continuous distribution on (0,∞)(0,\infty)(0,∞) whose survival function satisfies G(s+t)=G(s)G(t)G(s+t) = G(s)G(t)G(s+t)=G(s)G(t) is forced to be e−λxe^{-\lambda x}e−λx for some λ\lambdaλ. So the Exponential is the only memoryless continuous distribution. Memorylessness characterizes it.

The Poisson bridge

Poisson process with rate λ Arrivals land on a timeline such that the number of arrivals in any interval of length ttt is Pois(λt)\text{Pois}(\lambda t)Pois(λt), and counts in disjoint intervals are independent.

The definition speaks only the counting language (how many arrivals, a discrete number). The waiting language is one sentence away: "the first arrival has not happened by time ttt" is the same event as "zero arrivals landed in (0,t](0, t](0,t]". Identical events have identical probabilities, so

P(T1>t)=P(Nt=0)=e−λt(λt)00!=e−λt.P(T_1 > t) = P(N_t = 0) = e^{-\lambda t}\frac{(\lambda t)^0}{0!} = e^{-\lambda t}.P(T1​>t)=P(Nt​=0)=e−λt0!(λt)0​=e−λt.

The Exponential survival function falls out of a definition that never mentioned waiting: T1∼Expo(λ)T_1 \sim \text{Expo}(\lambda)T1​∼Expo(λ). After the first arrival, independence of disjoint intervals restarts the process fresh, so the gaps between arrivals are i.i.d. Expo(λ)\text{Expo}(\lambda)Expo(λ). (Disjoint is doing the work there: overlapping intervals share arrivals, so their counts are obviously dependent.) One process, two views: count the arrivals and you get Poisson, time them and you get Exponential. That is why they share the letter λ\lambdaλ.

The units wire I crossed twice before it settled: λ\lambdaλ is a rate (arrivals per unit time), E(T1)=1/λE(T_1) = 1/\lambdaE(T1​)=1/λ is a time. Reciprocals, not equals. Buses at rate 6 per hour have mean gap 1/61/61/6 hour, and 6≠1/66 \ne 1/66=1/6. They coincide only at λ=1\lambda = 1λ=1, the deceptive special case. The other λ\lambdaλ, the prefactor in λe−λt\lambda e^{-\lambda t}λe−λt, is the same letter doing a second consistent job: the density's per-time units showing up for work.

Bitcoin runs on this bridge. Block arrivals are engineered into a Poisson process (difficulty retargets to hold λ=1/10\lambda = 1/10λ=1/10 per minute), inter-block gaps are Expo(1/10)\text{Expo}(1/10)Expo(1/10), and memorylessness is what "progress-free mining" means. After 25 minutes without a block, the expected wait is still 10 minutes from now. A block is never "due".

What stayed shaky: Fred's bus paradox

Buses in Blotchville have Expo gaps with mean 10 minutes, Fred arrives at a random moment, and memorylessness resets the clock: his expected wait is a fresh 10 minutes.
